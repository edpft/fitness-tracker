### What the operator has said

> "we're not using toml as an input mechanism anymore" — 2026-09-02

> "I have repeated asked you to get rid of toml. I was even assured by a previous
> agent that we weren't using the toml crate at all any more. […] an agent decided

> written into a document and treated as gospel." — 2026-09-02

**He has asked for this repeatedly across sessions and it has not happened.** It
is the first thing, not a nice-to-have.

### What is actually there

```sh
grep -rn "toml" Cargo.toml crates/*/Cargo.toml     # the dependency
grep -rln "toml::" crates/*/src crates/*/tests     # the users
wc -l crates/infrastructure/src/programme/*.rs     # the size
```

| use | size | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| `crates/infrastructure/src/programme/document.rs` | 1,155 lines | **remove** |
| `crates/infrastructure/src/programme/draft.rs` | 568 lines | **remove** |
| `credentials.rs` — `credentials.toml`, `0600`, XDG | 2 calls | see below |
| `settings.rs` — `config.toml`, XDG | 2 calls | see below |

**Whether the two config files stay is an agent's call, never reviewed.** The
reasoning was that a dot-config file is a different thing from a programme input
format and he objected only to the latter. He has not been asked. **Ask him
before touching them, and ask before leaving them.**

### The path to cut

`crates/cli/src/wizard.rs`, `add()`: asks questions → builds a `Draft` → renders
TOML → **writes `<name>.toml` into the working directory** → `prescribing::add`
reads the file back → authors.

The wizard already holds every answer in typed form. The text layer is a
round-trip through a file that nobody asked for.

**What must survive is the validation, not the parsing.** `Document::programme()`
resolves the calendar, resolves fills against a predecessor, builds the anchor,
and applies each template's own rules (which fields it requires and which it
refuses). Extract that into a function over typed inputs; delete the text.

### What breaks

```sh
grep -rln "Document\|toml::from_str" crates/infrastructure/tests/
```

Four test files author through documents: `standalone_test.rs`,
`scheduled_programme.rs`, `authored_data.rs`, `sbs_cycle.rs`. They would build
programmes directly instead. The round-trip test added on 2026-09-02
(`the_wizard_s_sbs_output_authors_a_cycle`) becomes moot and goes with the format.

## 2. Cycling: a session is an intention plus zone minimums

### What the operator has said

> "in the cycling domain, I don't think we need to care about in workout power
> zone block structure. the important thing, for prescription, is that a workout
> has, for example, >= 9 minutes in Z4, >= 14 minutes in Z3, and >= 10 minutes in
> Z2, not that it follows a 5/7/9/7/5 pyramid shape." — 2026-09-02

> "it's not quite just time in zone, there's also an intention or training
> outcome that's being targeted. that's what we need to keep so that we can find
> classes that fit the intention" — 2026-09-02

### What that changes

`domain::cycling::session` currently holds `Ride::Intervals(NonEmpty<Interval>)`
— an ordered sequence. That sequence is **Peloton's realisation** of the
prescription, not the prescription. It goes, along with `Interval`.

A session becomes an **intention** plus **per-zone minimums**. The intention is
what makes matching possible: two classes with the same Z5 total can serve
different outcomes, and time-in-zone alone cannot tell them apart.

The zone totals already exist, computed and reconciled, in
`docs/cycling-peak-your-power-zones.md` under *Time in zone, per transcribed
class*.

### Open, and his to answer

**What the intentions are.** They have not been named. **Do not name them from
the data** — an earlier session grouped the classes into "endurance / threshold /
VO2 / anaerobic" from their zone profiles, which is a description of the sample,
not a vocabulary. Ask.

**Whether minimums are per-zone or cumulative from below** — does ten minutes at
Z5 satisfy "≥ 9 minutes Z4"? Raised on 2026-09-02, **not answered**.

### One observation that may or may not matter

Week 3 day 1 is ten 30-second Z5 bursts; week 5 day 1 is three sustained Z5
blocks of 2–3 minutes. Similar Z5 totals, different sessions. His answer about
intention probably covers this — but nobody has confirmed that it does.

## 3. Warm-up protocol and hold rules

### Verified against the published source

Read directly from `Program_Notes_and_Recommendations.pdf`, page 5, section 1.1
*AMAP Sets & Rep Maxes*. **This was not read until 2026-09-02** — the earlier
sessions worked from the spreadsheet alone, and the images had silently dropped
out of the first read.

> "Whenever you see an AMAP set (as many as possible), this should be taken to
> mean as many reps as possible with no form breakdown. If your lumbar spine
> starts rounding when deadlifting, if your butt is coming off the bench, or if
> your knees start caving or your butt shoots straight up in the air on the
> squat, that set is over."

**The stopping rule is form breakdown, not RIR 0.**

The rep-max table, verbatim: 10RM → 75%, 8RM → 80%, 5RM → 85%, 3RM → 90% of the
training max for the next week. Rounding, from the worked example: *"When it's
between two 5-pound increments, round down."*

**`training_max_share` is missing the 10RM row.** Verify:

```sh
grep -n "=> 10_000\|=> 9_000\|=> 8_500\|=> 8_000" crates/domain/src/prescription/sbs/chart.rs
```

It has 1, 3, 5 and 8. The published table has 10 at 75%. The chart in use does
not prescribe a 10RM, so nothing is currently wrong — but the table is
incomplete against its source.

### Decided, not implemented

> "okay, I think we hold, as it's the closest thing to what the SBS programme
> recommends" — 2026-09-02, on what to do when the target reps are missed

So: **attempting an 8RM and getting 7 leaves the training maximum unchanged.**
This matches SBS's own handling of the analogous AMAP case — *"if you get 8 or
fewer reps, leave your training max the same"*.

`sbs::chart::maximum_after` currently advances off any completed top set on a
gating day, with no check that the target repetitions were reached. **The hold
rule is not implemented.** It needs the performed rep count, which
`GatingTopSet` does not currently carry.

### Still open

**How to find the rep max.** The chart says `1×8 @ 8RM` — one set at a weight
that *is* the eight-rep maximum. If the estimate is soft and he gets ten, the
rule then counts a weight that is not his 8RM. **SBS's notes do not give a
work-up protocol.** His own suggestion, not settled:

> "do we then use the normal warm-up protocol then go for 8 reps with that
> weight, and if the last rep wasn't RIR 0, rest, add 2.5kg, and try again? do we
> need a different protocol for higher rep maxes?" — 2026-09-02

Note that "RIR 0" there is his framing and the published notes say *form
breakdown* instead. Worth putting to him.

**The warm-up ramp target is an agent's call, never reviewed.**
`PrimaryLoad::RepMax` builds toward the current maximum × SBS's share for that
rep count — the exact inverse of the advance the result will produce. It is a
defensible derivation and nobody has confirmed it is the right thing to warm up
toward.
