## What the tool is for

The operator plans a term. He gives a **span** — week commencing to week
commencing, w/c Monday 14 September through w/c Monday 14 December, fourteen
microcycles — the **providers** to draw on, the **primary lift**, and **how many
sessions per microcycle each discipline gets**. The tool already holds his
slots, his absences, and the rules for using them.

It returns **every arrangement whose fatigue profiles cohere**, and he picks one.
Then `fitness next` prescribes whatever is next, whichever discipline, reading
its loads from the record.

**The aim, his words**: *"to produce coherent hybrid programmes."*

**1.0.0 is the version that runs the autumn on this.** The crates are at 0.2.0.
Crossing 1.0.0 is a release choice, not a consequence of a breaking change.

## Vocabulary, settled and load-bearing

- **template** — Eugene Teo's sense, a heuristic that structures a workout.
  **Not built.** What exists is a *configured workout*: seventeen slots, an
  exercise in each. Do not call it a template.
- **provider** — who wrote the programming. SBS, Peloton, Friel, the old
  CrossFit gym. **The bounded context.**
- **programme** — one or more mesocycles. SBS is one of four; Peloton's Base and
  Peak are two of four; Build is five microcycles.
- **a test is not a programme.** It has no mesocycles, and it is never *placed*
  — it is derived, when a programme states an input the record cannot answer
  recently enough.
- **the linear holding pattern is not a programme.** No mesocycles. It is what
  runs when a proper multi-week programme cannot.
- **programming** is the act; a programme is one thing that act places. A
  **training plan** is what it produces.
- **levels**: session → microcycle → mesocycle → programme → macrocycle. A
  macrocycle divides into terms and holidays, which the school year decides, so
  they sit under § 14 beside the slots.

## Where the autumn stands

Fourteen microcycles, the first taken by a derived entry test, leaving thirteen.

**Gym**: three SBS cycles, microcycles 2–13, unloading at 5, 9 and 13.
Microcycle 14 is spare.

**Cycling**: two candidates, and with Build asked for four microcycles rather
than its written five, **both align three of three**:

- **Base (8) + Build (4)** — eight microcycles of aerobic engine, one FTP test
  at 13, the most volume and so the most interference with the gym.
- **Build (4) + Peak (8)** — FTP work throughout, tests at 5 and 13 so the zones
  track fitness, less volume, no aerobic base this term.

**The operator has not chosen.** The fatigue constraint no longer discriminates
between them, which is it working correctly; what remains is a training
judgement and it is his.

## What is needed from the operator, in order

1. ~~Which four-week Peloton programme~~ — **resolved**: there isn't one.
   Peloton has four programmes of 5, 8, 5 and 8 microcycles. The choice above
   replaces it, and the *pick between the two candidates* is still open.
2. **His current FTP, and the date it was tested.** § 13 makes FTP effect-dated,
   and the entry test week's other cycling session is zone-based, so it needs an
   FTP before the test produces a new one. **This is the next question to put to
   him.**
3. **The rep-max work-up ramp.** Research found no published protocol to adopt —
   the established ones are all 1RM testing, which treats a multiple-RM as a
   *predictor* and warns off anything above five reps, which is not what SBS
   does with it. The agreed shape is a submaximal warm-up, a ramp set near 80%
   for three, then attempts at 3–5 minutes' rest with a cap. **The increment
   near the target and the cap are his to state**, once, after which it is a
   constant (0028).
4. **Confirm the spacing rule.** Is 0018's "spacing rule" the same as his *"a
   full rest day before the hardest gym session"*? If yes it is already
   specified and can be built.

Answered on 2026-09-03 and recorded: zone minimums are **independent floors**
(ten minutes at Z5 does not satisfy "≥ 9 minutes Z4"); credentials go to the OS
keystore and settings to the database, with **no TOML anywhere**.

## What to build, and none of it waits on him

In rough order. `docs/removing-toml.md` has the survey and plan for the first.

3. **0028's split** — `Scales` stays a parameter; `WarmupStep`, `BackOff`,

   is no table, no application code and no command.
6. **Transcribe Build**, and Base if he picks that pairing. Record what it
   answers when asked for four microcycles as a **set** of options, not one.
7. **The planner, the span view, and `fitness next`.**

## Traps

- **`nix flake check` only sees tracked files.** `git add` before believing it.
- **Panics are `forbid`.** No `#[tokio::test]`, no clap derive macros.
- **Regenerate `.sqlx`** after changing a query.
- **A green gate is not a user-visible improvement.** Run against a copy of
  `local.db`, and `.backup` it rather than opening `mode=ro`, which returns a
  stale snapshot without erroring.
- **Decision records are for the next session, not for the operator.** He will
  not read them. Do not ask him to approve a status, a number or a version.
- **He cannot open repo files from where he works.** Send the file or raise a
  PR; a markdown link to a path is useless to him.
