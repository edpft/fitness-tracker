## What the tool is for

The operator plans a term. He gives a **span** — week commencing to week
commencing, w/c Monday 14 September through w/c Monday 14 December, fourteen
microcycles — the **providers** to draw on, the **primary lift**, and **how many
sessions per microcycle each discipline gets**. The tool already holds his
slots, his absences, and the rules for using them.

It returns **every arrangement whose fatigue profiles cohere**, and he picks one.
Then `fitness next` prescribes whatever is next, whichever discipline, reading
its loads from the record.

**The aim, his words**: *"to produce coherent hybrid programmes."*

**1.0.0 is the version that runs the autumn on this.** The crates are at 0.2.0.
Crossing 1.0.0 is a release choice, not a consequence of a breaking change.

## Vocabulary, settled and load-bearing

- **template** — Eugene Teo's sense, a heuristic that structures a workout.
  **Not built.** What exists is a *configured workout*: seventeen slots, an
  exercise in each. Do not call it a template.
- **provider** — who wrote the programming. SBS, Peloton, Friel, the old
  CrossFit gym. **The bounded context.**
- **programme** — one or more mesocycles. SBS is one of four; Peloton's Base and
  Peak are two of four; Build is five microcycles.
- **a test is not a programme.** It has no mesocycles, and it is never *placed*
  — it is derived, when a programme states an input the record cannot answer
  recently enough.
- **the linear holding pattern is not a programme.** No mesocycles. It is what
  runs when a proper multi-week programme cannot.
- **programming** is the act; a programme is one thing that act places. A
  **training plan** is what it produces.
- **levels**: session → microcycle → mesocycle → programme → macrocycle. A
  macrocycle divides into terms and holidays, which the school year decides, so
  they sit under § 14 beside the slots.

## What 2026-09-04 settled, and what it cost

All of it is PR #53, on `feat/the-work-up-descends-from-its-rep-count`.

**The autumn authors and tiles.** Proven on a copy of `local.db`, not argued:

```text
autumn-entry-test  test  2026-09-14  1 week   → 2026-09-20
sbs-1              sbs   2026-09-21  4 weeks  → 2026-10-18
sbs-2              sbs   2026-10-19  4 weeks  → 2026-11-15
sbs-3              sbs   2026-11-16  4 weeks  → 2026-12-13
```

Thirteen microcycles, all twelve SBS heavy sessions prescribing, each cycle
opening `tested` from the previous one's week-4 single. **The summer programme
runs to 2026-09-13, not the 6th**, so the entry test can only be w/c 14 September
— which is where this document already put it, and there is no room to put it
earlier.

**A cycle cannot open from nothing.** Without an asserted maximum the author is
refused: *"a programme that climbs opens from a maximum"*. The route without an
assertion is a **preceding test programme**; the cycle then opens `tested` from
its date, and the test does not need to have been performed — the succession
check asks whether the programme before it *produces* a maximum, not whether it
has yet.

**A session states the load it works to.** The repetition-maximum top set is a
set at a stated weight taken to nothing in reserve, not a work-up: decision
0030's ramp does the working up, so by the time the bar is loaded there is one
set left. The week-4 test states its target for the same reason — the ramp is
built as a share of exactly that number, and withholding it worked the operator
up to something the session had already decided. 0011 still keeps the target out
of `programme show`, where it is a projection for a week that has not happened.

**The rounding was losing ground (0031).** The maximum round-trips through the
plate grid every rep-max day, and flooring both ends lost at both ends: doing
exactly what the chart asked ran it 92.5 → 85 → 77.5 → 70 across the autumn.
`advance` now raises to the increment and `working_load` still floors. **This
departs from the published programme**, whose own rule has the same flaw hidden
by a round example. Say so if a later session finds it and thinks it is a bug.

## The FTP question was wrong

Written 2026-09-03, after the operator asked why the tool needs his zones at
all. It doesn't, and the code already said so.

**A cycling prescription is complete without an FTP.** `cli::cycling::next`
takes `ftp: Option<Ftp>`, and with `None` the session still renders in full —
warm up, the ordered intervals, time in zone, cool down, the Peloton class and
its link. FTP adds one thing: a watt range appended to each interval line, and
`report` deliberately prints nothing rather than a placeholder without it.

**The shape the operator wants is already the model.** In his words, a
prescription is *"duration in zones + some way of distinguishing between shorter
and longer intervals in those zones"*. `Ride::Intervals` is an ordered
`NonEmpty<Interval>` of zone × duration, so 6 × 30s at Z6 and 3 × 3min at Z5 are
already distinct; `time_in_zone` collapses them only for the summary printed
underneath.

**What made it look load-bearing was a doc comment, and the comment was mine.**
`cycling/zone.rs` says "the same class prescribes different watts before and
after a test", which assumes watts are part of the prescription. They are not.
The § 13 machinery hung off `Ftp` — the effect-date, the provenance — is
circular as a justification: FTP needs effect-dating *because* it would be a
stored interpretive parameter, which is not a reason to store one.

**On the bike the conversion is Peloton's.** It holds his FTP and its power bar
shows his zones. A second copy here diverges the moment a test updates one and
not the other.

**Where it will earn its place, and the operator's decision.** Reading the
record back — checking he held Z4 for the prescribed nine minutes — means
converting recorded watts to zones, which needs the FTP in force that day. His
ruling: *"it would be useful to know what my zones were at some point in time
but we can defer that until we read data from Peloton."* So the need is real and
it arrives with Peloton ingestion, not before. The types stay as they are; they
cost nothing and they are right when it comes.

Riding off Peloton — a power meter and no app — would also need watts, and is
hypothetical while the transcribed programme is Peloton classes.

## The 8RM anchor looks too heavy

Not settled, and not a question for the operator yet — it is a fact about the
record that whoever builds the anchor needs.

`repmax.rs` is the RTS grid's RIR-0 column, linear at 2.5 points per rep, so it
makes an 8RM 82.5% of the 1RM — 77.5kg at an e1RM of 92.5. **Every other
published equation comes in lower**, and the record lower still:

```text
RTS linear (what we use)   76.31kg   82.5% of 1RM
Brzycki                    74.50     80.5%
Mayhew (exponential)       73.25     79.2%
Epley                      73.03     78.9%
Wathan (exponential)       72.45     78.3%
arXiv 2603.17495 (2026)    70.70     76.4%
```

The record: **65 × 6 at RIR 2–3 on 2026-08-24** says eight or nine reps were
available at 65kg. **77.5 × 4 at RIR 1–2 on 2026-08-21** puts 77.5 nearer a
5–6RM. Both were back-off sets after a heavy top set, so a fresh 8RM sits above
65 — but not the 12.5kg above it that 77.5 would need.

**The common remedy is not a better formula; it is a closer set.** Prediction
holds up between roughly 2 and 10 reps and degrades outside it, so extrapolating
seven reps out from a single is the move the literature warns against whichever
equation is used. Derive each rep max from the part of the record nearest that
rep count — the 8RM from sets of five to eight, the 1RM from singles. That is
0027's rule applied one level deeper: not merely that the numbers come from the
record, but that they come from the *comparable* part of it.

Two related things to weigh when this is picked up: `best_of` in `wizard.rs`
takes an all-time best with no recency window, so the store's highest e1RM is
94.74 from 90 × 3 on **2025-04-28**; and the RTS grid is Reactive Training
Systems' sitting inside an SBS programme, which is the sort of crossing 0026
governs.
